# Research Principles

1. **Research before implementation.** Every indicator requires rationale, definition, source, units, frequency, transformation, interpretation, and limitations.
2. **Availability-time integrity.** Store and use publication timestamps and vintages where a source provides them. Historical research must only use information available at the decision time.
3. **Layer separation.** Raw observations, cleaned/processed observations, derived indicators, signals, model outputs, and decisions are distinct artifacts.
4. **Reproducibility.** Preserve source data and version transformations; a processed value must trace to its raw series.
5. **Independent evidence.** Organize indicators by conceptual factor and test incremental information rather than accumulating correlated measures.
6. **No automatic advice.** A descriptive or research signal is not a portfolio action without validation and an explainable decision rule.
7. **Validation.** Later research must include out-of-sample, walk-forward, costs, slippage, robustness, and regime-aware evaluation.
8. **Transparency.** Prefer interpretable definitions and methods; document all exceptions and changed definitions.
